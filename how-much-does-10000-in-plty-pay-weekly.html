What is $PLTY?

$PLTY is the YieldMax PLTR Option Income Strategy ETF. It launched on October 7, 2024, and it's built to pay weekly income off Palantir ($PLTR) while keeping some exposure to the stock's price.

This is the part most people miss: $PLTY doesn't own Palantir shares. Its holdings are mostly U.S. Treasuries and cash, plus $PLTR options that create a synthetic long position. On top of that it sells call spreads on $PLTR to collect premium. That premium, plus the Treasury interest, is what funds the weekly checks. The trade-off: you get only part of $PLTR's upside, and you take the downside.

  • Issuer: YieldMax (Tidal Investments)
  • Strategy: synthetic long $PLTR plus call spread writing, weekly distributions
  • Expense ratio: 1.07% (gross)
  • Net assets: about $319 million (November 2026)
  • Distribution rate: 46.40% as of November 11, 2026 (annualizes only the newest payout)
  • 30-day SEC yield: 2.60% as of October 31, 2026
  • Tax character: changes every week. The newest payout is estimated at 0% return of capital, the one before it at 100%.

When does $PLTY pay? The weekly schedule

$PLTY has paid every week since October 2025. Before that, it paid roughly every four weeks. YieldMax runs it on a tight rhythm: the distribution is declared on Wednesday, the ex-date is Thursday, and the cash pays on Friday. Holiday weeks shift a day or two (the Thanksgiving week in 2025 went ex on Friday and paid the following Monday).

To get a given week's check, you need to own shares before the ex-date, which means buying by Wednesday's close. For the full list of funds that pay this way, see my list of weekly paying ETFs for 2026.

Why did the payouts shrink? Mostly because the share price shrank. The fund pays out based on the option premium it collects, and premium is a percentage of a smaller and smaller share price. $PLTY peaked at a $94.87 close on February 18, 2025, bottomed at $26.24 on June 25, 2026, and sits around $31 now. The real test: total return (and the price decay)

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. Everything else is marketing.

$PLTY closed its first trading day (October 8, 2024) at about $51.43. It closed at $31.00 on November 12, 2026. That's down about 40%, while it paid out $82.03 per share in cash. That's why our live list shows price decay for $PLTY. And it happened while Palantir itself went from about $41 to about $172.

So $PLTY turned $10,000 into about $22,000 in a little over two years if you took the cash. That's a great result in isolation. You got back more than your whole investment in cash. But plain $PLTR stock turned the same $10,000 into about $41,400, almost double.

That gap is the cost of the strategy. When you sell calls on a stock that triples, you give away most of the upside. Then the fund pays out a big chunk of its value every week, so the share price never gets to compound. YieldMax's own numbers as of October 31, 2026: 192.92% cumulative since inception on NAV (with distributions reinvested), but -4.78% over the last year. Our live list shows about +161% since inception on its own methodology. Different windows, same conclusion: big early, rough lately, and well behind the stock.

The other risks: $PLTY is a single-stock fund. If $PLTR drops hard, $PLTY takes the full hit on the way down, and the option premium won't come close to covering it. It caps upside on the way up. The 1.07% expense ratio is high. Part of many distributions is estimated to be return of capital, which lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed right away, but also means some of the "income" is the fund handing your money back. And the payout can drop fast, as it did from about $0.73 in early September to $0.22 two weeks later.

Who $PLTY is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you want income from Palantir's volatility, you're fine giving up most of the big upside days, and you can live with a share price that falls over time. Think of it as a cash flow tool on one stock, not a paycheck replacement.
  • It doesn't fit if you need predictable income. The check went from $0.7253 to $0.2238 in two weeks this September. If you're budgeting off one payment, you'll get burned.
  • Reinvesting changes the math. Plowing the checks back in raises your share count and fights the price decay, but it also raises your exposure to one very volatile stock. Spending every check means watching the position shrink. I'd decide based on how much $PLTR risk you actually want.

For a steadier core, I'd rather build on diversified payers and keep single-stock funds like $PLTY as a small satellite. $PLTY questions, answered

How much does $10,000 in $PLTY pay per week?

At $31.00 per share (November 12, 2026 close), $10,000 buys about 322 shares of $PLTY. At the newest payout of $0.2761 per share that is about $89 for the week. At the 13-week average of about $0.383 per share it is about $124 per week. Payouts change every week and are not guaranteed.

What is $PLTY?

$PLTY is the YieldMax $PLTR Option Income Strategy ETF. It launched on October 7, 2024, holds mostly Treasuries plus Palantir ($PLTR) options that create synthetic long exposure, sells call spreads on $PLTR to generate income, and pays a distribution every week. The gross expense ratio is 1.07%.

What is $PLTY's dividend history?

$PLTY has paid 70 distributions from its first ex-date on November 7, 2024 through the one that went ex on November 12, 2026, about $82.03 per share in total: about $5.55 across 2 payouts in 2024, $57.96 across 22 payouts in 2025 and $18.51 across 46 weekly payouts in 2026. It switched from roughly every four weeks to weekly in October 2025.

What is the $PLTY dividend yield?

YieldMax's distribution rate, which annualizes only the newest payout, was 46.40% as of November 11, 2026. The last 52 payouts total about $23.13 per share, roughly 75% of the $31.00 share price, because that window includes much larger payouts from late 2025. Both figures move every week.

How often does $PLTY pay dividends?

Every week. YieldMax usually declares the distribution on Wednesday, the ex-date and record date are Thursday, and the cash is paid on Friday. Holiday weeks can shift by a day or two.
