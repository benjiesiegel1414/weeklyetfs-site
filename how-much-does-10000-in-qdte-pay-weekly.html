The quick answer

$QDTE closed at $25.44 on November 5, 2026, so $10,000 buys about 393 shares. The newest distribution is $0.158112 per share (declared November 4, ex-date November 5, paid November 6). That's roughly $62 for the week.

One week never tells the whole story, so I also look at the average. Over the last 13 weeks $QDTE paid about $0.1443 per share on average, which on 393 shares is about $57 a week, or around $246 a month. Over the last 20 weeks the average was higher, about $0.1635, because the summer checks were bigger.

So the honest range right now is about $54 to $64 a week on $10,000, depending on which stretch you use. That's real weekly cash from the Nasdaq-100, but the check moves every week, and it has been smaller this fall than it was in July and August.

What is $QDTE?

$QDTE is the Roundhill Nasdaq-100 0DTE Covered Call Strategy ETF. It launched on March 7, 2024 and trades on Cboe BZX. Until August 31, 2026 it was called the Roundhill Innovation-100 0DTE Covered Call Strategy ETF, so you'll still see the old name in a lot of places.

Here's the part most people miss: $QDTE doesn't own the 100 Nasdaq stocks. It gets its Nasdaq-100 exposure synthetically, through long-dated deep in-the-money call options on $QQQ, and then sells zero-days-to-expiry (0DTE) calls on the index to collect premium. Roundhill describes it as overnight exposure to the Nasdaq-100 plus income generated each morning. As of November 5, 2026 the top holdings were two $QQQ FLEX call positions (72.56% and 17.45%) and the Roundhill Weekly T-Bill ETF, $WEEK, at 5.84%.

  • Issuer: Roundhill Investments
  • Strategy: Synthetic Nasdaq-100 exposure, sell 0DTE calls daily, pay weekly
  • Gross expense ratio: 0.96%
  • Assets: about $821.88 million
  • Distribution rate: 32.43% as of November 5, 2026 (annualizes only the newest payout)
  • 30-day SEC yield: -0.65% as of October 31, 2026 (it leaves out option income)

$QDTE is one of three 0DTE funds in Roundhill's income lineup, next to $XDTE (S&P 500) and $RDTE (Russell 2000). I ranked the full lineup in Roundhill weekly ETFs 2026, and $QDTE also made my list of the 10 safest weekly dividend ETFs for passive income.

The real test: total return

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. A 32% yield means nothing if the price is melting.

On that test, $QDTE has done better than most people expect. Roundhill reports a 1-year return of 22.81% at market price (22.57% at NAV) and 21.62% a year since inception, both as of September 30, 2026, with distributions reinvested. That's a strong record for a fund paying out this much.

But look at the share price and you see the trade-off. Our list flags $QDTE for price decay, meaning the price tends to drift lower over time while the checks keep coming. And it can move fast: on November 5, 2026 alone, $QDTE's market price fell 6.71%. If you spend every check, you're likely living off a shrinking pile. If you reinvest, the total return is what you actually earn.

Of the three, $QDTE pays the most and has the best total return on our list. $XDTE pays about half as much but tracks a broader, steadier index. If you want weekly income with no price decay on our list, Roundhill's T-bill fund is the one that fits, and I covered it in the $WEEK ETF breakdown.

The risks: $QDTE is built on options, not stock ownership, so it can behave differently from $QQQ, especially in fast markets. Selling calls every day caps the upside on big up days, and the fund still takes the losses on down days. The weekly amount changes with Nasdaq volatility and has ranged from about $0.11 to $0.28 in just the last 20 weeks. Roundhill notes distributions may exceed income and gains and can be treated as return of capital, so check the supplemental tax info and your 1099.

Who $QDTE is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you want weekly cash tied to the Nasdaq-100 instead of a single stock, and you understand the option structure.
  • It doesn't fit if you need a fixed paycheck. The weekly amount has moved by more than 2x in a few months. Plan around the low end and treat anything above it as a bonus.
  • Reinvesting changes the math. With a fund that has a solid total return but a drifting price, reinvesting the weekly cash is what keeps your share count growing. I'd rather reinvest part of the checks here than spend all of it.

My takeaway

$10,000 in $QDTE pays about $57 to $62 a week right now, roughly $246 a month at the 13-week pace. The latest check was $0.158112 per share, and the next ex-date is November 12, paying November 13.

The total return has been better than most 30%-plus yielders, and that's the reason I take $QDTE more seriously than a lot of single-stock weekly funds. But the price decay is real and the check is not fixed. I treat it as a Nasdaq position that spins off weekly cash, not a bond replacement. Keep it a sensible size, decide on purpose whether you're reinvesting, and judge it on total return, not the yield.
