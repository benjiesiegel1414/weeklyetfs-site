The quick answer

$FBY closed at $7.87 on November 6, 2026, so $10,000 buys about 1,270 shares. The newest distribution is $0.0493 per share (ex-date November 5, paid November 6). That's roughly $63 for the week.

One check is a snapshot, not a paycheck, so I always look at the average too. Over the last 13 weeks $FBY paid about $0.0746 per share on average, which on 1,270 shares is about $95 a week, or around $411 a month. Over the last 52 weeks the average was about $0.0847, which works out to roughly $108 a week.

So the honest range right now is about $63 to $108 a week on $10,000, depending on which stretch you use. The latest check happens to be near the bottom of that range, and that's the thing to understand about this fund: the weekly amount swings a lot with Meta's volatility.

What is $FBY?

$FBY is the YieldMax META Option Income Strategy ETF. It launched on July 27, 2023, trades on NYSE Arca, and is built around one stock: Meta Platforms ($META).

Here's the part most people miss: $FBY doesn't really own Meta shares. Look at the holdings and you'll see roughly 90% of the fund sitting in U.S. Treasury notes and bills, plus cash. The Meta exposure comes from options. YieldMax describes the current strategy as selling call spreads on $META to harvest option premium every week while keeping some participation in Meta's upside. The Treasuries hold the money, the options create the Meta exposure and the income.

  • Issuer: YieldMax ETFs
  • Strategy: Option-based $META exposure, sells call spreads for income, pays weekly
  • Gross expense ratio: 1.06%
  • Net assets: about $94.14 million (November 6, 2026)
  • Distribution rate: 32.09% as of November 5, 2026 (annualizes only the newest payout)
  • 30-day SEC yield: 3.04% as of October 31, 2026 (it leaves out option income)

$FBY used to pay monthly. Like most of the YieldMax single-stock lineup, it moved to weekly distributions in October 2025, which is why it now shows up on the WeeklyETFs.com master list. I covered the whole family in YieldMax ETFs for high weekly income.

The real test: total return

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. A 32% yield means nothing if the price is melting faster than the checks come in.

$FBY's longer record is decent. YieldMax reports 14.56% a year at market price since inception and 15.24% a year over 3 years, both as of October 31, 2026, with distributions included. That's real money for a fund that pays out this much.

The last year is a different story. Over the 52 weeks to November 5, 2026, $FBY paid about $4.40 per share in distributions. That's more than half of today's $7.87 share price. And yet the 1-year total return was -11.14% at market price as of October 31, 2026. Put those together and you see what happened: the share price fell by more than all of those checks combined. If you spent every distribution, your position shrank a lot.

That's what price decay looks like in practice, and the WeeklyETFs.com list flags $FBY for it. The fund still shows a 53% total return since inception on our list, so the early years did most of the heavy lifting.

The risks: $FBY is a single-stock fund, so everything rides on Meta. Selling call spreads limits how much of a big Meta rally you keep, while the fund still takes most of the downside when Meta drops. The weekly amount has moved by more than 3x in a few months, and many payouts are estimated to be largely return of capital, which can lower the share price over time. Check the 19a-1 notices and your 1099 for the final tax character.

Who $FBY is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you're bullish to neutral on Meta, want weekly cash from it, and you're fine giving up some of the upside for that income.
  • It doesn't fit if you need a steady paycheck. Plan around the low end, roughly $60 a week per $10,000 right now, and treat anything above that as a bonus.
  • Reinvesting changes the math. With a fund whose price drifts lower, reinvesting part of the checks is what keeps your share count, and your future income, from shrinking. I'd rather reinvest some of the cash here than spend all of it.
  • Size matters. I treat a single-stock option fund like a satellite position, not the core of an income portfolio. A diversified weekly payer or a plain dividend ETF should do the heavy lifting.

My takeaway

$10,000 in $FBY pays about $63 a week at the latest check and about $95 a week at the 13-week pace, roughly $411 a month. The next ex-date is November 12, paying November 13.

The income is real, but so is the price decay. Over the past year the fund paid out more than half its current share price and still lost money on a total return basis. That doesn't make $FBY useless. It makes it a tool: Meta exposure that spins off weekly cash, with a capped upside and a share price that tends to drift down. Keep it a sensible size, decide on purpose how much you reinvest, and judge it on total return, not the yield. $FBY questions, answered

How much does $10,000 in $FBY pay per week?

At $7.87 per share (November 6, 2026 close), $10,000 buys about 1,270 shares of $FBY. At the latest distribution of $0.0493 per share that is about $63 per week. At the 13-week average of about $0.0746 per share it is about $95 per week, or roughly $411 per month. Distributions change every week and are not guaranteed.

What is $FBY?

$FBY is the YieldMax META Option Income Strategy ETF. It launched on July 27, 2023, holds mostly U.S. Treasuries and gets its Meta Platforms exposure through options, selling call spreads on $META to generate weekly income. The gross expense ratio is 1.06%.

When does $FBY pay dividends?

$FBY pays weekly. YieldMax usually declares on Wednesday, the ex-dividend date is Thursday, and it pays on Friday. Upcoming ex-dates are November 12, November 19, November 27 (Thanksgiving week, paying November 30) and December 3, 2026.
