The quick answer

Weekly dividend ETFs come in seven basic types, and the type tells you more than the yield does. From safest to wildest: T-bill funds like $WEEK (~3.6%), index option funds like $XDTE and $QDTE (~10% to 30%), basket funds like $CHPY and $YMAX (~20% to 60%), Roundhill WeeklyPay 1.2x single-stock funds (~19% to 59%), YieldMax single-stock funds (~18% to 100%), boosted funds like GraniteShares YieldBOOST (~26% to 77%), and crypto income funds (~36% to 53%).

Here's the one stat I'd tape to your monitor: on our list of 100+ weekly payers, only 4 show no price decay: $WEEK, $CHPY, $GOOW and $AMDW. Everything else has given back some share price to pay those checks.

How to read this cheat sheet

Every table below uses the same three numbers from the WeeklyETFs.com master list, updated daily:

  • Yield: the current annualized distribution yield. On weekly option funds this moves a lot, so treat it as a range, not a promise.
  • Price decay: whether the share price has eroded since launch. "No" is what you want.
  • Total return: price change plus every distribution, since inception. This is the number that tells you if the fund actually made you money.

This is the part most people miss. A 60% yield with a negative total return means you got your own money back with extra steps. I always check total return before I even look at the yield. If you're brand new to this, start with how weekly dividend ETFs work.

My 5 rules for picking a weekly dividend ETF

  • Total return first, yield second. If total return since launch is negative, the yield was an illusion.
  • Know what's underneath. A T-bill, an index, a basket and a single meme stock are four completely different risks, even if they all pay weekly.
  • Watch price decay. Only 4 funds on our list show none right now. That's a short list worth knowing.
  • Size by risk. Core income in the lower-risk types, small satellite bets in Types 5 to 7.
  • Mind the taxes. Many weekly payouts include return of capital or are taxed as ordinary income. Check your 1099 or use an IRA.

My takeaway

The fastest way to make sense of weekly dividend ETFs is to stop sorting by yield and start sorting by type. T-bills and index funds are your foundation. Basket funds like $CHPY can be a strong middle layer. WeeklyPay and YieldMax single-stock funds are bets on one company. Boosted and crypto funds are the high-risk corner.

I've been investing for income for over a decade, and the people who do well with weekly payers are the ones who care about keeping their principal, not just the size of Friday's deposit. Weekly dividend ETF questions, answered

What ETFs pay weekly dividends?

Weekly dividend ETFs fall into seven types: T-bill funds like $WEEK, index option funds like $XDTE, $QDTE and $RDTE, basket funds like $CHPY and $YMAX, Roundhill WeeklyPay 1.2x single-stock funds like $NVDW and $GOOW, YieldMax single-stock funds like $NVDY and $MSTY, boosted funds like GraniteShares YieldBOOST, and crypto income funds like $YBTC and $BLOX. The WeeklyETFs.com list tracks 100+ of them daily.

Which weekly dividend ETFs have no price decay?

As of October 24, 2026, only four weekly payers on the WeeklyETFs.com list show no price decay: $WEEK (Roundhill Weekly T-Bill ETF), $CHPY (YieldMax Semiconductor Portfolio Option Income ETF), $GOOW (Roundhill GOOGL WeeklyPay ETF) and $AMDW (Roundhill AMD WeeklyPay ETF).

What is the safest weekly dividend ETF?

The lowest-risk weekly payer is $WEEK, which holds U.S. Treasury bills with 0 to 3 months to maturity and yields about 3.6%. Among higher-yield options, diversified index funds like $XDTE and $QDTE spread risk across hundreds of stocks instead of one company. No ETF is risk-free and this is not financial advice.

What is the best weekly dividend ETF for passive income?

There is no single best one. For passive income I look at total return first and yield second. On our list, $CHPY stands out with about a 40% yield, no price decay and a 139% total return since its April 2025 launch, while $QDTE and $XDTE are diversified index options. Do your own research before investing.

How much do I need to invest to make $100 a week in dividends?

$100 a week is $5,200 a year. At a 20% yield you would need about $26,000, at a 40% yield about $13,000, and at a 3.6% T-bill yield about $144,000. Higher yields usually carry more price decay risk, and payouts change every week.
