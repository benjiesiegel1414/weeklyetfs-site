What is the $WEEK ETF?

$WEEK is the Roundhill Weekly T-Bill ETF. It started trading on March 6, 2025 on Cboe BZX, and it does one simple thing: it buys short-term U.S. Treasury bills and pays the interest out every week.

This is the part most people miss when they see it on a list of weekly payers. $WEEK sells no options. There's no covered call, no swap, no single stock. It's the weekly version of a T-bill fund. Roundhill says it's the first ETF of its kind that targets a stable NAV week over week, meaning each week's payout is sized so the share price stays about flat.

  • Issuer: Roundhill Investments
  • Holds: U.S. Treasury bills maturing in 0 to 3 months
  • Expense ratio: 0.19%
  • Assets: about $183 million
  • Management: active
  • Options on $WEEK: not available yet

That design is why $WEEK ranked first in my list of the 10 safest weekly dividend ETFs. Safe is relative, but nothing else on WeeklyETFs.com comes close on stability.

Look at the maturities. There's a bill coming due basically every week for the next three months. That's the engine: one bill matures, the cash rolls into a new 3-month bill, and the interest gets paid out. It's a T-bill ladder you don't have to manage. And because nothing in here matures more than about three months out, rate changes barely move the share price.

$WEEK yield and total return

There are three ways to quote the yield, and for once they all agree:

  • Distribution rate: 3.73% (October 30, 2026). The newest payout annualized.
  • 30-day SEC yield: 3.67% (October 31, 2026). The standardized income yield after expenses.
  • Last 52 payouts: about 3.5% of the $100.03 price.

When those three numbers line up, the payout is real income, not your own money coming back. Compare that with a single-stock fund where the distribution rate is 60% and the SEC yield is under 1%.

Who $WEEK is for (and who it isn't)

  • It makes sense for your emergency fund, cash you'll need in the next year, or the "dry powder" part of an income portfolio. You get T-bill income, paid weekly, with a share price that barely moves.
  • It also works as a ballast next to high-yield weekly funds. If most of your weekly income comes from option funds with price decay, a slice of $WEEK smooths out the ride.
  • It doesn't fit if you're trying to grow wealth or need a big yield. Over the long run stocks have beaten T-bills by a mile, and inflation eats into a 3.7% yield.

Personally, I've never treated cash as an investment. It's a tool. But if I'm going to hold cash anyway, I'd rather it pay me every week than sit at 0% in a checking account.

The risks (yes, even for T-bills)

What can go wrong: The payout falls when short-term rates fall, and it already has since 2025. Inflation can outrun a 3.7% yield. Roundhill notes that distributions may exceed income and be treated as return of capital, and the stable NAV is a target, not a guarantee. It's a young, actively managed, non-diversified fund with about $183 million in assets. T-bills are backed by the U.S. government, but the ETF itself is not a bank account and isn't FDIC insured.

On taxes: interest from Treasury bills is generally exempt from state and local income tax, which helps in high-tax states, but how a fund's distributions qualify depends on your state and the fund's year-end tax reporting. Check your 1099 and talk to a tax pro.

My takeaway

$WEEK pays about $0.07 per share every week, a 3.73% distribution rate, from a ladder of Treasury bills that all mature within about three months. $10,000 pays roughly $7 a week or $353 a year. The share price has stayed right around $100 since launch, and it's one of the only weekly payers with no price decay on our list.

It's not a yield play and it's not a growth play. It's the safest way I know to get paid weekly on cash. If you're building a weekly income portfolio, I'd use $WEEK for the money that can't take a hit, and keep the higher-yield funds for the part that can. $WEEK ETF questions, answered

Does the $WEEK ETF pay a dividend every week?

Yes. The Roundhill Weekly T-Bill ETF ($WEEK) pays a distribution every week. It is usually declared on Monday, goes ex-dividend on Tuesday and is paid on Wednesday. The latest payout was $0.071837 per share, ex-date October 27, 2026, paid October 28. The next one goes ex on November 3, 2026 and pays November 4.

What is the $WEEK ETF dividend yield?

Roundhill listed a 3.73% distribution rate as of October 30, 2026 and a 3.67% 30-day SEC yield as of October 31, 2026. The last 52 weekly payouts total about $3.54 per share, roughly 3.5% of the $100.03 share price. The yield moves with short-term Treasury bill rates.

What does the $WEEK ETF hold?

$WEEK holds only U.S. Treasury bills maturing in 0 to 3 months. As of November 1, 2026 it held 13 T-bills maturing between November 3, 2026 and January 26, 2027, each about 7.6% to 7.8% of the fund. It does not sell options or hold stocks.

How much does $10,000 in $WEEK pay per week?

At $100.03 per share (October 30, 2026 close), $10,000 buys about 100 shares. At the latest $0.071837 payout that is about $7.18 a week, and the last 52 payouts add up to about $353 a year. Payouts change weekly and are not guaranteed.

Is $WEEK ETF a good investment?

$WEEK is built for cash you want to keep stable while earning T-bill income weekly. It has a 0.19% expense ratio, no price decay on the WeeklyETFs.com list, and returned 3.62% at NAV over the year to September 30, 2026. It will not grow like a stock fund and its payout falls when rates fall. This is not financial advice; do your own research.
