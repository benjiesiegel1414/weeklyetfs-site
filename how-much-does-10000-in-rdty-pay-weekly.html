The quick answer

$RDTY closed at $31.96 on November 23, 2026, so $10,000 buys about 313 shares. The newest distribution was $0.2489 per share (declared November 17, ex-date November 18, paid November 19). On 313 shares that's roughly $78 for the week, or about $337 a month if it held there.

What is $RDTY?

$RDTY is the YieldMax R2000 0DTE Covered Call Strategy ETF. It launched on March 5, 2025 and it's part of YieldMax's "Lite" lineup, the funds built to balance income with some upside instead of squeezing out the biggest possible yield.

Here's how it works. $RDTY gets exposure to the price return of the Russell 2000 Index, the benchmark for U.S. small caps, through a synthetic covered call strategy. On top of that it sells out-of-the-money, zero days to expiration (0DTE) call options on the index. Those options expire the same day they're sold, so the fund can collect premium over and over, and that premium is what gets paid out every week.

This is the part most people miss: because the calls are out of the money and expire daily, $RDTY keeps some of the index's upside on most days. But YieldMax is clear that its gains are limited while it's exposed to all of the index's losses, which the option income may not offset.

  • Issuer: YieldMax ETFs (adviser: Tidal Investments)
  • Strategy: Synthetic Russell 2000 exposure plus daily 0DTE out-of-the-money calls, weekly distributions
  • Expense ratio: 1.73% (gross)
  • Net assets: about $27.1 million (850,000 shares, as of November 23, 2026)
  • Distribution rate: 39.62% as of November 17, 2026 (annualizes only the newest payout)
  • 30-day SEC yield: -0.90% as of October 31, 2026

Two of those numbers deserve a flag. The 1.73% expense ratio is high for an index fund, even an options one. And the negative SEC yield just means the fund's regular interest and dividend income didn't cover its expenses that month. The option premium isn't counted in that number at all, so it doesn't tell you much about the weekly check. If you like the 0DTE idea on the Nasdaq-100 instead, I ran the same math in how much $10,000 in $QDTE pays per week.

When does $RDTY pay? The weekly schedule

$RDTY runs on YieldMax's Lite fund rhythm, which is one day earlier than the single-stock YieldMax funds: the distribution is declared on Tuesday, the ex-date and record date are Wednesday, and the cash pays on Thursday. To get a week's check, you need to own shares before Wednesday's open, which means buying by Tuesday's close.

Holiday weeks shift things. Last Thanksgiving the payout was declared Tuesday, November 25, 2025, went ex on Wednesday the 26th and paid on Friday the 28th. If YieldMax follows that pattern this week, expect a declaration around Tuesday, November 24, an ex-date around Wednesday, November 25, and payment after the holiday. That's my read of last year's pattern, not an announcement, and the amount isn't known until it's declared. Recent weeks have had odd dates too (the November 12 payout went ex on a Thursday, and the November 4 payout was declared and went ex on the same day), so always check YieldMax's distribution page before trading around one.

The real test: total return vs the Russell 2000

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. And for an index-based fund, the fair comparison is simple: did it beat just owning the index?

$RDTY closed its first trading day (March 6, 2025) at about $49.21. It closed at $31.96 on November 23, 2026. That's down about 35%, while it paid about $26.61 per share in cash along the way. Over the same stretch the iShares Russell 2000 ETF ($IWM) went from about $205.28 to $265.74, up about 29% on price alone.

So $RDTY made money, and it turned the Russell 2000 into a weekly paycheck. But it trailed plain small caps by roughly 7 points with reinvesting, and by more if you spent the checks. That's the price of selling calls: you collect steady premium, and in return you give up part of the big up days.

YieldMax's own month-end numbers tell the same story. Through October 31, 2026, $RDTY returned 16.53% year to date and 14.10% over one year at NAV, and 15.60% a year since inception. Our live list, which counts reinvested distributions, shows about +24% since launch. Want to line it up against other funds? ETFTotalReturns.com runs that comparison.

The risks: $RDTY is still a small cap equity fund, so a Russell 2000 selloff hits it directly, and the option income may not cover the loss. The 0DTE calls cap upside on strong days. The 1.73% expense ratio is steep. Most 2026 payouts were estimated to be partly or fully return of capital, which is why the share price has fallen from about $49 to about $32. And the fund is small, about $27 million, with a 0.35% median bid-ask spread, so use limit orders.

Who $RDTY is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you want small cap exposure and would rather get paid every week than wait for growth. The checks are steadier than single-stock funds because they come from an index, not one company.
  • It doesn't fit if you want maximum long-term growth. Plain $IWM beat it over the same period, with a far lower fee.
  • Think about reinvesting. Here, reinvesting helped: about +25% vs +19% for taking the cash. If you don't need the income yet, a plain index fund might do the job better and cheaper.

For me, a fund like $RDTY belongs in the income bucket, not the growth bucket. I'd rather know exactly what I'm giving up (some upside, a 1.73% fee) for the weekly cash. Monthly payers are on MonthlyETFs.com, and every high yielder's dividend history is on TopDividendETFs.com.

I'd treat $RDTY as a way to turn small cap exposure into weekly cash flow, not as a replacement for owning the index.
