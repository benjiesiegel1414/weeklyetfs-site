The quick answer

$PLTW closed at $24.97 on October 29, 2026, so $10,000 buys about 400 shares. On October 30 Roundhill declared the next distribution: $0.344074 per share (ex-date November 2, paid November 3). On 400 shares that's roughly $138 for the week.

What is $PLTW?

$PLTW is the Roundhill PLTR WeeklyPay ETF. It started trading on February 19, 2025 as one of the first five WeeklyPay funds, and it tries to give you 1.2 times (120%) the calendar week total return of Palantir ($PLTR), plus a distribution every week.

This is the part most people miss: $PLTW does not sell covered calls. Roundhill says it outright: WeeklyPay ETFs don't sell options. The fund gets its Palantir exposure mostly through total return swaps, adds a little leverage, and pays out cash each week using a formula based on $PLTR's performance and implied volatility. You keep the upside on Palantir, with 1.2x leverage. The trade-off is a payout that moves around a lot.

  • Issuer: Roundhill Investments
  • Strategy: 1.2x weekly exposure to $PLTR, weekly distributions, no options sold
  • Expense ratio: 0.99%
  • Assets: about $133 million (October 29, 2026)
  • Distribution rate: 71.65% as of October 29, 2026 (annualizes only the newest payout)
  • Tax character: 100% estimated return of capital, per Roundhill's latest 19a-1 notice

It's one of a whole family of single-stock WeeklyPay funds. I ranked all of them on total return and price decay in the best WeeklyPay ETFs to buy in 2026, and the full Roundhill shelf is in the Roundhill WeeklyPay ETFs distributions and performance guide.

Why did the payouts shrink? Two reasons. First, $PLTW's share price fell from the $50s in the summer of 2025 to a low of about $15 in late June 2026. The same payout rate on a $25 share is a much smaller dollar check than on a $50 share. Second, the payout also depends on $PLTR's implied volatility, which cooled off from its 2025 extremes. The catch: the share price and the total return

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. Everything else is marketing.

$PLTW closed its first trading day (February 19, 2025) at $44.24. It peaked at a $57.75 close on August 8, 2025, bottomed near $15.16 on June 25, 2026, and closed at $24.97 on October 29, 2026. That's down about 44% from day one. But the fund paid out about $37.82 per share in cash along the way, so the price chart only tells half the story.

So $PLTW made money, about 42% in roughly 20 months if you took the cash. But plain $PLTR stock did a lot better, even though $PLTW is built to deliver 1.2x of Palantir's weekly moves.

That gap is volatility drag. Palantir is one of the most volatile large caps out there, and resetting 1.2x leverage every week while paying out cash every week chips away at compounding when a stock whipsaws. Timing matters too. Roundhill's own numbers as of September 30, 2026: -7.21% over one year and 21.59% a year annualized since inception. Our live list shows about +64% since inception on its own methodology. Different start points and methods, same conclusion: positive, but well behind the stock.

The other risks: $PLTW can lose all of its value if $PLTR drops 83.33% in a single calendar week (Roundhill's own disclosure). It's one stock, it's leveraged through swaps, so there's counterparty risk too, and the distributions are estimated to be 100% return of capital. That lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed as income right away, but it also means a big part of the "income" is the fund handing your money back. If you won't watch it, this isn't the fund.

Who $PLTW is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you're bullish on Palantir, want weekly cash flow while you wait, and can stomach 10% moves in a day. Think of it as a leveraged $PLTR position that spins off cash, not a paycheck replacement.
  • It doesn't fit if you need predictable income. A $46 week was followed by a $138 week. And the 2026 average check is less than half of 2025's. If you're budgeting off this, you'll get burned.
  • Reinvesting changes the math. Plowing the checks back in raises your share count, but it also raises your exposure to one very volatile stock. Spending every check from a fund with price decay means watching the position shrink. I'd decide based on how much Palantir risk you actually want.

For a steadier core, I'd rather build on diversified payers and keep single-stock funds like $PLTW as a small satellite. My takeaway

$10,000 in $PLTW pays about $138 this week and has averaged about $109 a week over the last 13 weeks. A year ago the same position threw off far more, because the share price was twice as high. Since launch the fund has made money, roughly +42% with the cash in your pocket, but it trailed plain $PLTR stock by a wide margin.

I treat funds like this as a tool, not a retirement plan. $PLTW questions, answered

How much does $10,000 in $PLTW pay per week?

At $24.97 per share (October 29, 2026 close), $10,000 buys about 400 shares. At the newest declared payout of $0.344074 per share that's about $138 for the week. At the 13-week average of about $0.27 per share it's about $109 a week. Payouts change every week and aren't guaranteed.

What is $PLTW?

$PLTW is the Roundhill PLTR WeeklyPay ETF. It started trading February 19, 2025, targets 1.2x the calendar week total return of Palantir ($PLTR) using swaps, and pays a distribution every week. It doesn't sell options. The expense ratio is 0.99%.

What is $PLTW's dividend history?

$PLTW has paid 88 weekly distributions from its first ex-date on March 3, 2025 through the one going ex on November 2, 2026, about $38.16 per share in total. That's about $27.03 across 44 payouts in 2025 and $11.12 across 44 payouts in 2026, ranging from $0.074026 to $1.585951 per share.

What is the $PLTW dividend yield?

It depends on the method. Roundhill's distribution rate, which annualizes only the newest payout, was 71.65% as of October 29, 2026. The last 52 payouts total about $15.46 per share, roughly 62% of the $24.97 price. Both numbers move every week.

Is $PLTW a good investment?

It's a leveraged single-stock income fund, so it fits investors who are bullish on Palantir and can handle big swings. From its $44.24 first close to $24.97, the share price fell about 44%, but including distributions a $10,000 buy was worth about $14,192, while $PLTR itself turned $10,000 into about $17,528.
