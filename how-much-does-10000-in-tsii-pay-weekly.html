$10,000 in the REX TSLA Enhanced Growth & Income ETF pays about $121 a week at the newest payout and about $104 a week at its 13-week average. Here's the full math, all 76 weekly distributions since launch, and how it stacks up against just owning Tesla.

The quick answer

$TSII closed at $12.16 on November 25, 2026, so $10,000 buys about 822 shares. The newest distribution was $0.1467 per share (declared November 23, ex-date November 24, paid November 25). On 822 shares that's roughly $121 for the week, or about $523 a month if it held there.

Over the last 13 weeks $TSII averaged about $0.1267 per share, which works out to about $104 a week, or roughly $452 a month. That's the number I'd plan around.

Here's the catch, and it's a big one. The checks have been shrinking. $TSII averaged about $0.28 a week in 2025 and about $0.18 a week in 2026, and the share price has roughly halved since launch. The weekly income on a fixed number of shares is a lot smaller than it was a year ago.

What is $TSII?

$TSII is the REX TSLA Enhanced Growth & Income ETF from REX Shares. It launched on June 4, 2025, and it's one of three funds in REX's Enhanced Growth & Income lineup, next to $NVII (Nvidia) and $WMTI (Walmart).

The pitch is right on REX's fund page: half uncapped upside, half weekly income from a covered call strategy. In plain English, $TSII sells calls on only part of its Tesla exposure, so the other part can still run if $TSLA rips. Most single-stock income funds write calls against everything, which caps the whole position.

There's a second piece most people miss. $TSII isn't a 1x fund. Its secondary objective is daily results between 105% and 150% of Tesla's daily move, before fees, and REX listed the leverage at 1.25 as of November 24, 2026. That's more upside on good days, and more pain on bad ones.

Look at the holdings and you can see how it's built. As of November 24, 2026, about 97% of the fund sat in Treasury bills. The Tesla exposure comes from options: a long December 18, 2026 $375 call paired with a short $375 put on the same date (that combo acts like owning the stock), plus a short $385 call expiring November 27 that generates the weekly premium.

  • Issuer: REX Shares
  • Strategy: 1.05x to 1.50x daily Tesla exposure through options, covered calls on part of the position, weekly distributions
  • Expense ratio: 1.52% (gross)
  • Fund assets: about $28.7 million (2,330,000 shares, as of November 24, 2026)
  • Distribution rate: 61.29% as of November 23, 2026 (annualizes only the newest payout)
  • 30-day SEC yield: 2.78% as of October 31, 2026
  • Median bid/ask spread: 0.31% (30 day)

The 1.52% fee is the number I'd flag first. On $10,000 that's about $152 a year, before any of the option costs inside the fund. It's a small fund too, under $30 million, so use limit orders when you buy or sell.

When does $TSII pay? The weekly schedule

$TSII runs on a Monday to Wednesday rhythm: the distribution is declared on Monday, the ex-date and record date are Tuesday, and the cash pays on Wednesday. 69 of its 76 ex-dates so far landed on a Tuesday. To get a week's check, you need to own shares before Tuesday's open, which means buying by Monday's close.

REX publishes its calendar ahead of time. The next one on the schedule is declared Monday, November 30, goes ex on Tuesday, December 1 and pays on Wednesday, December 2, 2026. The amount isn't known until it's declared. Holiday weeks move around: REX's calendar shows two ex-dates in Christmas week (December 22 and December 24), with the second one paying December 28.

What about return of capital? REX says its current distributions consist of 94.4% estimated return of capital, and it points to the fund's 19a-1 notices for the full breakdown. ROC isn't automatically bad, since it lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed right away. But when nearly the whole check is ROC and the share price keeps sliding, a big part of that "yield" is your own money coming back. You can see the live yield on the $TSII fund page.

The real test: total return vs just owning Tesla

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. For a single-stock fund the fair comparison is simple: did it beat just owning the stock?

$TSII closed its first trading day (June 4, 2025) at $24.25. It closed at $12.16 on November 25, 2026. That's down about 50%, while it paid about $16.60 per share in cash along the way. Over the same stretch $TSLA went from about $332.05 to $380.14, up about 14.5%. Tesla pays no dividend, so that's its whole return.

So this one is closer than you'd think. If you took every check as cash, $TSII roughly kept pace with Tesla, even a bit ahead, while paying you every single week. If you reinvested, you did worse, because you kept buying shares that kept falling. That's the price decay trap in one row of a table.

But the timing matters a lot here. REX's own numbers through September 30, 2026 show -23.19% year to date and -19.75% over one year at NAV, and 4.59% since inception. Most of the good stretch came in 2025. Our live list, which counts reinvested distributions, shows about +5% since launch. Want to line it up against other funds? ETFTotalReturns.com runs that comparison.

The risks: $TSII is a leveraged bet on one stock. With 1.05x to 1.50x daily exposure, a bad Tesla week hits harder than owning the shares, and REX warns the fund can lose money even if $TSLA is flat or up over time because of daily rebalancing and compounding. The covered calls cap part of the upside. The 1.52% expense ratio is steep. The current payouts are estimated at 94.4% return of capital, and the share price has fallen from $24.25 to about $12. REX itself says the fund isn't meant for investors who don't actively monitor it.

Who $TSII is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you're bullish on Tesla, you want to get paid every week while you wait, and you're fine checking the position often. The partial call writing leaves more room for upside than most Tesla income funds.
  • It doesn't fit if you want a set-it-and-forget-it income stream. Leverage plus one volatile stock is the opposite of that, and REX says so directly.
  • Think hard about reinvesting. Here, taking the cash beat reinvesting by about 7 points, because reinvested checks bought a falling share price. If you don't need the income, owning the stock was simpler and cheaper.

For me, a fund like $TSII is a satellite, not a core holding. I'd size it so a 50% drop wouldn't change my life, and I'd rather build the base of my income on steadier payers. Monthly payers are on MonthlyETFs.com, and every high yielder's dividend history is on TopDividendETFs.com.

My takeaway

$10,000 in $TSII pays about $121 this week and has averaged about $104 a week over the last 13 weeks. Since launch it has paid 76 weekly checks worth about $16.60 per share, but the checks in 2026 have been much smaller than in 2025.

The cash-taken total return has held up close to Tesla itself, which is better than a lot of single-stock income funds can say. But the share price is down about 50% from launch, the 2026 returns are negative, nearly all of the current payout is estimated return of capital, and the leverage cuts both ways. I'd treat $TSII as a high-risk way to get paid on a Tesla view, not as an income foundation. If you own it, plan with the 13-week average and check the total return every month. $TSII questions, answered

How much does $10,000 in $TSII pay per week?

At $12.16 per share (November 25, 2026 close), $10,000 buys about 822 shares of $TSII. At the newest payout of $0.1467 per share (ex-date November 24, 2026) that is about $121 for the week. At the 13-week average of about $0.1267 per share it is about $104 per week, roughly $452 a month. Payouts change every week and are not guaranteed.

What is $TSII?

$TSII is the REX TSLA Enhanced Growth & Income ETF from REX Shares. It launched on June 4, 2025. Its primary objective is weekly distributions, and its secondary objective is daily results between 105% and 150% of Tesla's daily move, before fees. It writes covered calls on part of its Tesla exposure and leaves the rest uncapped. The gross expense ratio is 1.52%.

What is $TSII's dividend history?

$TSII has paid 76 weekly distributions from its first ex-date on June 17, 2025 through November 24, 2026, about $16.60 per share in total: about $8.00 across 29 payouts in 2025 and $8.59 across 47 payouts in 2026. The biggest was $0.5325 (ex-date November 18, 2025) and the smallest was $0.0886 (September 3, 2025).

What is the $TSII dividend yield?

REX's distribution rate, which annualizes only the newest payout, was 61.29% as of November 23, 2026. The 30-day SEC yield, which excludes option income, was 2.78% as of October 31, 2026. REX estimates the current distributions are 94.4% return of capital.

When does $TSII pay dividends?

Every week. REX usually declares the $TSII distribution on Monday, the ex-date and record date are Tuesday, and the cash pays on Wednesday. The next one on REX's calendar goes ex on December 1, 2026 and pays December 2. Holiday weeks can shift the dates.
