Look at that last column. Some of these are monster winners. $AMDW is up about 215% with every payout counted. Others, like $MARO and $COIW, are down 70% or more after the income. Same 70% to 80% yield. Completely different outcome.

How do these ETFs pay 50% to 100%+ yields?

No company on earth pays a 100% dividend. These funds manufacture the income, and there are basically three ways they do it:

  • Selling options on volatile stocks. YieldMax funds like $CVNY (Carvana) and $MRNY (Moderna) sell options on one stock and pay out the premium. The more volatile the stock, the bigger the premium, and the bigger the yield.
  • Leverage plus a payout formula. Roundhill's WeeklyPay funds, like $ARMW and $AMDW, aim for about 1.2x the weekly return of one stock and pay a weekly distribution based on that stock's moves and volatility. No covered calls, so no capped upside, but no cushion either.
  • Short exposure. $FIAT (short Coinbase) and $WNTR (short Strategy) earn income while betting a stock goes down. When that stock rips higher, they get hurt.

Here's the thing most people miss. A big yield isn't free money, it's volatility turned into cash. And a lot of that cash is your own money coming back to you as return of capital.

The distribution rate vs the SEC yield

The "yield" on high income ETFs is almost always the distribution rate: the newest payout annualized. It's not what the fund actually earns in interest and dividends. That's the 30-day SEC yield, and the gap is wild:

How I'd actually use a high yield ETF list

I've been a dividend investor for over 10 years, and I reached financial freedom by building income. I still don't build the core of my income on 100% yields. Here's the filter I run, in this order:

  • Total return first. If a fund has lost money since launch even with every check counted, it hasn't done its job yet. I'd rather own something with a lower yield and a positive total return.
  • Price decay second. Almost all of these have it (110 out of 113). The question is how fast. A fund down 10% from launch is a different animal than one down 80%.
  • Then look at what it holds. One volatile stock? An index? A short bet? Would I be fine owning that stock for three years with no income at all?
  • Size it small. I treat single-stock high yield ETFs like a satellite position, never the foundation. The foundation is boring dividend ETFs that grow their payout over time.

The risks with any high yield ETF

  • Return of capital: much of the payout on many of these funds is your own money returned, not profit.
  • Price decay: 110 of 113 funds on my list are below their launch price.
  • Single-stock risk: most of the top 30 track one company. If it crashes, the fund crashes, and the payouts shrink with it.
  • Leverage and swaps: WeeklyPay funds use about 1.2x exposure, which amplifies drops and adds counterparty risk.
  • High fees: expense ratios around 1.00% to 1.40% are normal here, versus 0.19% for $WEEK.
  • Payouts change every week: the yield you see today is not a promise for next week.
  • Taxes: option income is generally taxed as ordinary income in a taxable account, so check with a tax pro before you buy.

My take

A high yield ETF list is a great starting point and a terrible shopping list. The top of the list changes every week, and the yield number tells you more about how volatile a stock is than about how good the fund is.

If I were building around these today, I'd start with the funds that have a positive total return and a stock I'd want to own anyway, keep them small, and let a core of steady dividend payers do the heavy lifting. That's how you get the weekly checks without waking up one day to find your principal cut in half.

High yield ETF questions, answered

What is the highest yielding ETF right now?

On the WeeklyETFs.com list checked November 22, 2026, the Roundhill ARM WeeklyPay ETF ($ARMW) had the highest yield at about 107%, and Roundhill showed a 128.86% distribution rate as of November 20, 2026. $CVNY, $FIAT and $WNTR followed at about 101%. These rates annualize recent payouts, change every week and are not guaranteed.

Which high yield ETFs have actually made money?

Since inception, the high yield ETFs (50% yield or more) with the best total returns on the list were $AMDW (+215%), $AMDY (+194%), $PLTY (+137%), $AMZY (+95%), $SNOY (+89%), $WNTR (+70%) and $MRNY (+61%). But 27 of the 44 funds yielding 50% or more had a negative total return since launch.

Are high yield ETFs a good investment?

They can be a tool, not a foundation. Of 113 weekly paying ETFs on the list, 110 had lost share price since launch and 56 had a negative total return. A huge yield usually comes from selling options on volatile stocks or from leverage, and much of the payout is often return of capital. Size them small and judge them on total return, not yield.

Why is the distribution rate so much higher than the SEC yield?

The distribution rate annualizes the newest payout and counts option income and return of capital. The 30-day SEC yield only counts net investment income. For example, Roundhill showed $AMDW with a 113.62% distribution rate as of November 20, 2026, but a 30-day SEC yield of 2.21% as of October 31, 2026.

What is the safest high yield ETF?

There is no safe 50%+ yield. Among weekly payers, the lowest risk option on the list is the Roundhill Weekly T-Bill ETF ($WEEK), with a 3.82% distribution rate as of November 20, 2026 and no price decay. For higher income with less single-stock risk, index-based funds like $XDTE and $QDTE held up better than single-stock funds.
