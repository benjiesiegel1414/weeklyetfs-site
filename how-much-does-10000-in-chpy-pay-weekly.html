The quick answer

$CHPY closed at $60.70 on October 20, 2026, so $10,000 buys about 165 shares. The newest distribution is $0.4702 per share (declared October 20, ex-date October 21, paid October 22). That's roughly $77 for the week.

One week never tells the whole story, so I also look at the average. Over the last 13 weeks $CHPY paid about $0.5467 per share on average, which on 165 shares is about $90 a week, or around $390 a month. The last 52 payments added up to $27.66 per share, which would be roughly $4,556 a year on a $10,000 position at today's price.

The good news compared to a lot of weekly payers: $CHPY's checks are fairly steady. Since launch, the smallest payout was $0.3454 and the biggest was $0.7754. That's a range of a little over 2x, not the 17x swings you see in some single-stock funds.

What is $CHPY?

$CHPY is the YieldMax Semiconductor Portfolio Option Income ETF. It launched on April 2, 2025, trades on NYSE Arca, and owns a basket of roughly 15 to 30 semiconductor stocks directly. On top of those shares it sells call spreads to collect option premium, and it pays that income out every week.

This is the part most people miss about $CHPY: it's not a synthetic single-stock fund. It actually holds the chip stocks, and because it sells call spreads instead of plain covered calls, it keeps some room to participate when semis rip. That design is a big reason the share price has held up better than most high-yield weekly payers.

  • Issuer: YieldMax ETFs
  • Strategy: Own 15 to 30 semiconductor stocks, sell call spreads, pay weekly
  • Gross expense ratio: 1.03%
  • Net assets: about $1.27 billion (October 20, 2026)
  • Top holdings (Oct 21, 2026): $NVDA 6.70%, $AMD 6.08%, $MRVL 5.94%, $AVGO 5.12%, $MU 4.75%, $LRCX 4.74%, $INTC 4.66%
  • Distribution rate: 40.15% as of October 20, 2026 (annualizes only the newest payout)

It's one of the bigger funds in the YieldMax lineup. I compared the whole family in YieldMax ETFs: the high-yield weekly lineup for 2026, and $CHPY also made my list of the 10 safest weekly dividend ETFs for passive income.

The real test: what did $10,000 actually return?

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. A 40% yield means nothing if the price is melting.

So let's run the last year. $CHPY closed at $58.33 on October 20, 2025. A year later it closed at $60.70. The price went up about 4% while the fund paid out $27.66 per share. That's not something you can say about most 40%-yielding funds.

That's a strong year. $CHPY captured most of the chip rally while handing you about 47% of your money back in cash along the way. It still trailed plain $SMH by a few points, which is exactly what you'd expect: selling call spreads caps part of the upside in a monster year.

Timing matters a lot here too. YieldMax reports a 1-year market price return of 90.90% and 155.85% cumulative since inception, both as of September 30, 2026. Then semis pulled back in October. $CHPY closed as high as $89.44 on June 22, 2026, and today's $60.70 is about 32% below that peak.

The risks: $CHPY is a sector fund, so it rises and falls with semiconductors, one of the most volatile corners of the market. In a chip selloff the price drops and the option premium may not cover it. YieldMax's estimates show return of capital anywhere from 0% to 100% of a given week's payout (the October 21 check was estimated at 23.12% ROC), which lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed as income right away. And the fund only has about 18 months of history, all during a strong market for chips.

I track this pattern across the whole category in weekly dividend ETFs with the lowest price decay. $CHPY is one of the few high yielders that has actually grown its share price, which is why my own data flags it with no price decay right now.

Who $CHPY is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you want semiconductor exposure and you'd rather get paid weekly than wait for growth. It's a real basket of chip stocks, not a bet on one ticker.
  • It doesn't fit if you need your principal to be stable. A 32% drop from the June high happened in four months. If that would make you sell, size it small or skip it.
  • Reinvesting changes the math. With a fund that has held its price, reinvesting the weekly cash compounds your share count nicely. Just remember you're also adding more semiconductor risk every week. I'd rather reinvest into a fund like this than chase a higher-yielding fund with a falling price.

How much does $10,000 in $CHPY pay per week?

What is $CHPY?

$CHPY is the YieldMax Semiconductor Portfolio Option Income ETF. It launched on April 2, 2025, holds roughly 15 to 30 semiconductor stocks such as $NVDA, $AMD, $MRVL and $AVGO, sells call spreads on them to generate option income, and pays distributions weekly. The gross expense ratio is 1.03%.

Does $CHPY pay dividends weekly?
