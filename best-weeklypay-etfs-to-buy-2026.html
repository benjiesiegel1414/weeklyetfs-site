The quick answer

WeeklyPay ETFs are Roundhill's line of funds that each target about 1.2 times the weekly total return of one stock (like Apple, Nvidia or Palantir) and pay a distribution every week. They're ETFs, not stocks, even though a lot of people search for them as "WeeklyPay stocks."

The best WeeklyPay ETFs so far have been the ones tied to stocks that kept going up. On our live list, $AMDW (+258% total return since inception) and $GOOW (+72%) are the only two WeeklyPay funds with no price decay. Behind them: $PLTW (+65%), $NVDW (+51%) and $AAPW (+41%).

And here's the part most people miss: the WeeklyPay funds with the biggest yields have mostly been the worst investments. $COIW yields 61% and is down 54% on total return. $MSTW yields 38% and is down 77%.

What are WeeklyPay ETFs?

Roundhill launched the first five WeeklyPay ETFs on February 19, 2025, tied to Nvidia, Tesla, Apple, Coinbase and Palantir. Today Roundhill lists 24 WeeklyPay tickers, including single stocks like $AMDW, $GOOW and $AVGW, a gold fund ($GLDW), a gold miners fund ($GDXW), a Treasury bond fund ($TSYW), and $TOPW, which holds a basket of the others.

Here's how a single-stock WeeklyPay fund works, in plain English:

  • 1.2x weekly exposure. Each fund targets about 120% of its stock's total return for the calendar week, Friday close to Friday close. If Apple rises 2% in a week, $AAPW aims for about 2.4% before costs. If Apple falls 2%, $AAPW aims for about a 2.4% drop.
  • Swaps and options, not shares. Roundhill gets the exposure through swap agreements and FLEX options. You don't own the stock, and you don't get its regular dividend.
  • A weekly payout. The fund pays a distribution every week. Roundhill says distributions can exceed the fund's income and gains, and that part is treated as return of capital.
  • The reset. The 1.2x target resets every week. Over months, your result will not be 1.2x the stock's return. Choppy markets eat into it.
  • Fees. The single-stock funds charge about 1% a year. $AVGW, for example, lists a 1.00% gross expense ratio.
The risk Roundhill spells out: because of the leverage, a WeeklyPay fund could lose all of its value if its stock falls 83.33% or more in a single calendar week. That's extreme, but it tells you what kind of product this is. It's a leveraged stock bet that pays weekly, not a bond fund.

How I'd pick a WeeklyPay ETF

I've been a dividend investor for over 10 years, and I've learned to ignore the yield column first. Here's what I actually look at, in order:

  • 1️⃣
    Would I own the stock anyway? If I wouldn't hold Coinbase or Strategy through a 50% drawdown, I have no business holding 1.2x of it. Start with a company you actually believe in.
  • 2️⃣
    Total return, not yield. A 40% yield that comes with a 50% price drop is a loss. Check the total return column before anything else.
  • 3️⃣
    Price decay. If the share price keeps sliding while the checks come in, a chunk of your "income" is your own money coming back. Only $AMDW and $GOOW pass this test right now. I track the full no-decay list in weekly dividend ETFs with the lowest price decay.
  • 4️⃣
    Fund size. Some WeeklyPay funds are tiny. Small funds can have wider bid/ask spreads and a higher chance of closing. I'd rather stick to the ones with real assets.
  • 5️⃣
    Taxes. Return of capital lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed right away. That's fine, but know it's happening, and check your 1099-DIV.

Is there a WeeklyPay index fund?

Sort of. $TOPW, the Roundhill Top WeeklyPay ETF, holds the WeeklyPay funds tied to the 25 biggest U.S. companies and tracks the Solactive Roundhill WeeklyPay Universe Index, reweighted every quarter. It started trading on September 4, 2025 as $WPAY and was renamed $TOPW on March 20, 2026.

The appeal is obvious: you spread the single-stock risk across a basket. The expense ratio is 0.32% net (0.61% gross, with a fee waiver through at least May 1, 2027). On our list it yields about 25%, shows price decay, and is up just 2% since inception. Diversified, yes. A winner so far, not really.

Who WeeklyPay ETFs are for (and who they aren't)

  • They can make sense as a small, high-conviction position on a stock you already want to own, if you like getting paid weekly and you understand you're taking 1.2x the risk.
  • They don't fit as a core income holding, a retirement paycheck, or a replacement for something like $SCHD. One bad quarter in the underlying stock can wipe out a year of payouts.
  • Reinvesting matters. If you spend every weekly check from a fund with price decay, you're watching your position shrink. I'd rather reinvest a good chunk or keep these small.

My takeaway

WeeklyPay ETFs are leveraged single-stock bets that pay you every week. The best ones so far, $AMDW and $GOOW, are the only two with no price decay on our list, and they won because AMD and Alphabet won. The worst ones had the highest yields and lost investors 37% to 77%.

So if you're asking which WeeklyPay "stock" to buy, flip the question around: which stock would you be comfortable holding with 1.2x leverage through a rough patch? Start there, check total return and price decay, keep the position size sensible, and treat the weekly check as a bonus, not the reason. WeeklyPay ETF questions, answered

What are WeeklyPay ETFs?

WeeklyPay is Roundhill Investments' family of ETFs that each target about 1.2 times the weekly total return of one stock or asset, measured Friday close to Friday close, and pay a distribution every week. Roundhill lists 24 WeeklyPay tickers, from $AAPW (Apple) and $NVDW (Nvidia) to $GLDW (gold) and $TOPW, a fund that holds the other WeeklyPay ETFs. They get their exposure with swaps and FLEX options, not by simply owning the stock.

What are the best WeeklyPay stocks to buy?

WeeklyPay funds are ETFs, not stocks, so the better question is which ones held up. On the WeeklyETFs.com list as of October 28, 2026, $AMDW (+258% since inception) and $GOOW (+72%) are the only two WeeklyPay funds with no price decay, followed by $PLTW (+65%), $NVDW (+51%) and $AAPW (+41%). The highest yielders, like $COIW at 61%, have some of the worst total returns. This is not financial advice; do your own research.

Are WeeklyPay ETFs leveraged?

Yes. Each single-stock WeeklyPay ETF targets about 120% (1.2x) of its stock's calendar-week total return, so a 1% weekly drop in the stock means about a 1.2% drop in the fund before costs. Roundhill warns a fund could lose all of its value if the stock falls 83.33% or more in a single calendar week.

Is there a WeeklyPay index fund?

Yes. $TOPW, the Roundhill Top WeeklyPay ETF, tracks the Solactive Roundhill WeeklyPay Universe Index, which holds the WeeklyPay ETFs tied to the 25 largest U.S. companies, reweighted quarterly. It launched on September 4, 2025 as $WPAY and was renamed $TOPW on March 20, 2026. Its net expense ratio is 0.32% (0.61% gross, waiver through at least May 1, 2027).

How often do WeeklyPay ETFs pay?

Every week. That's the whole point of the line. The amounts change week to week with the stock's moves, and Roundhill notes distributions can include return of capital, which lowers your cost basis.
