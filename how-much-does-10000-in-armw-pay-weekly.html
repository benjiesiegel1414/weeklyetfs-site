What is $ARMW?

$ARMW is the Roundhill ARM WeeklyPay ETF. It launched on October 23, 2025 and tries to give you 1.2 times (120%) the calendar week total return of Arm Holdings ($ARM), plus a distribution every single week.

This is the part most people miss: $ARMW does not sell options. It's not a covered call fund like most high-yield weekly payers. It gets its exposure to $ARM through total return swaps, with a little leverage on top, and Roundhill calculates each weekly payout with a formula based on $ARM's performance and implied volatility. So you keep the upside on $ARM (with 1.2x leverage), and you also get paid every week. The trade-off is that the payout itself is very unpredictable.

  • Issuer: Roundhill Investments
  • Strategy: 1.2x weekly exposure to $ARM, weekly distributions, no options sold
  • Expense ratio: 1.00%
  • Assets: about $32.8 million (October 2026)
  • Distribution rate: 33.79% as of October 16, 2026 (annualizes only the latest payout)

It's part of a whole family of single-stock WeeklyPay funds. I broke down the rest in the Roundhill WeeklyPay ETFs distributions and performance guide and the high yield Roundhill weekly ETFs list.

The catch: the share price and the total return

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. Everything else is marketing.

$ARMW closed its first day at $50.87. Today it's about $32.67, down roughly 36%. That looks ugly, but it's not the whole story, because the fund paid out $33.46 per share along the way.

So $ARMW made money. About 30% in under a year is a great result. But look at the right column: plain $ARM stock did better, even though $ARMW is supposed to deliver 1.2x of $ARM's weekly moves.

That's volatility drag. When a stock swings as wildly as $ARM did this year (it more than tripled off its lows into June, then gave a big chunk back), resetting leverage every week and paying out cash every week chips away at the compounding. The bigger the swings, the bigger the drag. And timing matters a lot: Roundhill reported a 75.97% return since inception as of September 30, 2026, before $ARM's October pullback.

The other risks: $ARMW can lose all of its value if $ARM drops 83.33% in a single calendar week (Roundhill's own disclosure). It's one stock, it's leveraged, and it has a one-year track record. Per Roundhill's latest 19a-1 notice, the estimated distribution composition is 100% return of capital, which lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed as income right away. If you're not willing to watch it, this isn't the fund.

I covered how common this pattern is in 20 weekly dividend ETFs down the most from their launch price. Price down, income up, and the total return is what decides whether you actually won.

Who $ARMW is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you're bullish on $ARM, want to get paid weekly while you wait, and can handle 10% moves in a day. Think of it as an $ARM position that spins off cash, not a bond replacement.
  • It doesn't fit if you need a predictable paycheck. A $65 week came just two weeks after a $280 week. If you want steadier income, I'd rather build the core on diversified payers and keep single-stock funds like $ARMW as a small satellite.
  • Reinvesting changes the math. Plowing the weekly cash back in raises your share count, but it also raises your exposure to one very volatile stock. I'd decide on reinvesting based on how much $ARM risk you want, not on the yield.

My takeaway

$10,000 in $ARMW pays somewhere between about $65 and $187 a week right now, depending on whether you use the latest check or the recent average, and it has ranged far wider than that. The fund has made money since launch, but it trailed plain $ARM stock, and the share price is down about 36% from where it started.

I treat funds like this as a tool, not a retirement plan. If you own it, own it because you want leveraged $ARM exposure with weekly cash flow, size it small, and track the total return, not the yield. That's the habit that keeps income investors out of trouble.

How much does $10,000 in $ARMW pay per week?

At about $32.67 per share on October 19, 2026, $10,000 buys roughly 306 shares. At the latest payout of $0.210789 per share that's about $65 per week. At the 13-week average of about $0.61 per share it's about $187 per week. Payouts change every week and aren't guaranteed.

What is $ARMW?

$ARMW is the Roundhill ARM WeeklyPay ETF. It launched October 23, 2025, targets 1.2x the calendar week total return of $ARM using swaps, and pays a distribution every week. It doesn't sell options. The expense ratio is 1.00%.

Does $ARMW pay a dividend every week?

Is $ARMW's distribution return of capital?
