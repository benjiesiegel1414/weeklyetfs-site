The quick answer

$AIPI closed at $35.22 on October 23, 2026, so $10,000 buys about 284 shares. The newest distribution is $0.2396 per share (declared October 20, ex-date October 21, paid October 22). That's roughly $68 for the week.

One week never tells the whole story, so I also look at the average. Over the last 13 weeks $AIPI paid about $0.2460 per share, which on 284 shares is about $70 a week, or around $303 a month. Over the last 12 months it paid $13.43 per share in total, which works out to about $3,813 a year on a $10,000 position at today's price.

The nice thing about $AIPI since it went weekly: the checks are boring in a good way. All 22 weekly payouts landed between $0.2317 and $0.2640. That's a tight range for a fund yielding around 35%.

What is $AIPI?

$AIPI is the REX AI Equity Premium Income ETF. It launched on June 4, 2024, trades on Nasdaq, and puts at least 80% of its assets in the stocks of the BITA AI Leaders Select Index, a basket of companies tied to artificial intelligence. On top of those shares it writes covered calls on individual stocks with high implied volatility, and pays the income out to you.

This is the part most people miss: $AIPI didn't start as a weekly payer. It paid monthly for its first two years, then switched to weekly distributions in May 2026. REX says that was a change in frequency, not in rate. You can see it in the numbers: the last monthly check was $1.0518, and four weekly checks of about $0.25 add up to roughly the same thing.

  • Issuer: REX Shares
  • Strategy: Own AI leaders, sell covered calls on the most volatile ones, pay weekly
  • Expense ratio: 0.65%
  • Net assets: about $474 million, 88 holdings (October 22, 2026)
  • Top holdings (Oct 22, 2026): $PLTR 11.17%, $NVDA 9.75%, $TSM 7.59%, $MRVL 6.74%, $NOW 3.77%, $IBM 3.65%, $STX 3.46%
  • Distribution rate: 34.79% as of October 20, 2026 (annualizes only the newest payout)

It sits in the "basket" bucket on my weekly dividend ETF cheat sheet, next to funds like $CHPY, $GPTY and its REX sibling $FEPI. The big difference from a fund like $CHPY: $AIPI sells plain covered calls, not call spreads, so it gives up more upside when its stocks rip.

The real test: what did $10,000 actually return?

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. A 35% yield means nothing if the price is melting.

So let's run the last year. $AIPI closed at $43.75 on October 23, 2025. A year later it closed at $35.22. The price fell about 19.5% while the fund paid out $13.43 per share.

So you made money, but look at how. You collected about $3,070 in cash and your shares lost about $1,950 in value. That's the classic high-yield trade: a chunk of the "income" is really your own share price coming back to you.

For context, REX's own numbers as of June 30, 2026 show a 1-year NAV return of 19.02% for $AIPI versus 22.32% for the S&P 500, and 19.64% since inception versus 19.93%. Close, but it hasn't beaten just owning the index. And on our live WeeklyETFs.com list, $AIPI shows a 35% yield, price decay, and a 53% total return since launch.

The risks: $AIPI is a concentrated AI fund. $PLTR and $NVDA alone are over 20% of it, so when AI stocks sell off, this drops hard (its NAV fell 3.16% in a single day on October 22, 2026). Covered calls cap your upside in big rallies, so you get most of the downside and only part of the upside. The payout has stepped down over time, from about $1.48 a month in 2024 to about $1.05 a month now. And REX estimates current distributions as 100% return of capital, which lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed as income right away.

I track this pattern across every weekly payer in weekly dividend ETFs with the lowest price decay. $AIPI isn't on the no-decay list, so weigh that against the yield before you buy it for income.

Who $AIPI is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you already want AI exposure and you'd rather collect a steady weekly check than wait on growth. It owns real AI stocks, not a synthetic bet on one ticker, and the weekly payouts have been very consistent.
  • It doesn't fit if you need your principal to hold steady. The share price fell about 19.5% in a year that still ended positive on total return. If that would make you sell, size it small or skip it.
  • Reinvesting changes the math. Reinvesting the weekly cash pushed the 1-year result from about +11% to about +15%. I'd rather reinvest into a fund like this than spend every dollar, because spending it all means watching your share value shrink.

My takeaway

$10,000 in $AIPI pays about $68 to $70 a week right now, roughly $303 a month, and the last 12 months of payouts would be about $3,813 on a $10,000 position at today's price. Since the switch to weekly in May 2026, the checks have been remarkably steady.

But steady checks and a growing account are two different things. Over the last year the price fell about 19.5%, and total return still came out positive only because the payouts were so big. I treat $AIPI as an AI position that spins off cash, not a bond replacement. Keep it a sensible size, reinvest some of it if you can, and judge it on total return, not the yield. $AIPI questions, answered

How much does $10,000 in $AIPI pay per week?

At $35.22 per share (October 23, 2026 close), $10,000 buys about 284 shares of $AIPI. At the latest distribution of $0.2396 per share that is about $68 per week. At the 13-week average of about $0.2460 per share it is about $70 per week, or roughly $303 per month. Distributions change and are not guaranteed.

What is the $AIPI ETF?

$AIPI is the REX AI Equity Premium Income ETF. It launched on June 4, 2024, trades on Nasdaq, invests at least 80% of its assets in stocks of the BITA AI Leaders Select Index such as $PLTR, $NVDA and $TSM, and writes covered calls on individual stocks with high implied volatility. The expense ratio is 0.65%.

Does $AIPI pay dividends weekly?

Yes, since May 2026. $AIPI paid monthly from July 2024 through April 2026, then switched to weekly distributions, a change in frequency rather than rate. It has paid 22 weekly distributions from May 27 through October 21, 2026, ranging from $0.2317 to $0.2640 per share.

What is the $AIPI dividend yield?

REX lists a 34.79% distribution rate as of October 20, 2026, which annualizes the latest payout. The last 12 months of distributions totaled $13.43 per share, about 38% of the $35.22 share price. REX estimates current distributions as 100% return of capital.

Is $AIPI a good investment?

It depends on your goals. From an October 23, 2025 close of $43.75 to $35.22 a year later, a $10,000 investment returned about +11% including distributions without reinvesting (about +15% reinvested), even though the share price fell about 19.5%.
