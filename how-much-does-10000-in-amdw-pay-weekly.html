What is $AMDW?

$AMDW is the Roundhill AMD WeeklyPay ETF. It started trading on July 24, 2025, and it tries to give you 1.2 times (120%) the calendar week total return of Advanced Micro Devices ($AMD), plus a distribution every week.

This is the part most people miss: $AMDW does not sell covered calls. Roundhill says WeeklyPay ETFs don't sell options and offer uncapped, levered upside instead. The fund gets its $AMD exposure mostly through total return swaps, and the weekly payout comes from a formula based on $AMD's performance and implied volatility. You keep all of $AMD's upside, with 1.2x leverage. The trade-off is a check that moves around a lot.

  • Issuer: Roundhill Investments
  • Strategy: 1.2x weekly exposure to $AMD, weekly distributions, no options sold
  • Expense ratio: 1.00% (gross)
  • Assets: about $144 million (November 10, 2026)
  • Distribution rate: 52.28% as of November 10, 2026 (annualizes only the newest payout)
  • 30-day SEC yield: 2.21% as of October 31, 2026
  • Tax character: 100% estimated return of capital, per Roundhill's most recent 19a-1 notice

It's part of a big family of single-stock WeeklyPay funds. I ranked them on total return and price decay in the best WeeklyPay ETFs to buy in 2026, and the full Roundhill shelf is in the Roundhill WeeklyPay ETFs distributions and performance guide.

When does $AMDW pay? The weekly schedule

Roundhill runs $AMDW on a tight weekly rhythm: the distribution is declared on Friday (Thursday in holiday weeks), the ex-date and record date are Monday, and the cash pays on Tuesday. To get a given week's check, you need to own shares before Monday's open, which means buying by Friday's close.

Roundhill's published calendar has the next ones lined up: declared November 13, ex November 16, paid November 17; then November 20, 23 and 24; then November 27, 30 and December 1.

Why did the payouts grow? Mostly because the share price grew. The formula pays out based on the fund's value and $AMD's implied volatility, so the same payout rate on a $90 share is a much bigger dollar check than on a $45 share. Look at the spring of 2026: when $AMDW sank to about $39 in late March, the checks shrank to $0.38 to $0.52. When it ran past $100 in June, they jumped to $1.82 to $2.08. The real test: total return (and no price decay)

Here's what I actually look at before I buy any weekly payer: total return. Share price change plus every distribution. Everything else is marketing.

$AMDW closed its first trading day (July 24, 2025) at about $51.58. It bottomed at a $38.99 close on March 30, 2026, peaked at $114.84 on June 30, 2026, and closed at $77.30 on November 10, 2026. That's up about 50% from day one, while paying out $60.38 per share in cash. That's why our live list shows no price decay for $AMDW, which almost never happens with a single-stock weekly payer.

So $AMDW turned $10,000 into about $26,700 in roughly 15 months if you took the cash. That's a monster result. But plain $AMD stock still did better, about $31,800, even though $AMDW is built to deliver 1.2x of $AMD's weekly moves.

That gap is volatility drag. $AMD swung hard on the way up. $AMDW went from $38.99 in late March to $114.84 at the end of June, back above $100 in October, then down to $58.18 on November 5 before bouncing to $77.30. Resetting 1.2x leverage every week while paying cash out every week chips away at compounding when a stock whipsaws like that. Roundhill's own numbers as of September 30, 2026: 341.98% over one year and 253.43% a year annualized since inception on market price. Our live list shows about +230% since inception on its own methodology. Different windows, same conclusion: huge, but still behind the stock.

The other risks: $AMDW can lose all of its value if $AMD drops 83.33% in a single calendar week (Roundhill's own disclosure). It's one stock, it's leveraged through swaps, so there's counterparty risk too, and the distributions are estimated to be 100% return of capital. That lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed as income right away, but it also means a big part of the "income" is the fund handing your money back. The no price decay label is a result of $AMD's huge run, not a guarantee. If $AMD rolls over, this fund falls 1.2x as hard and keeps paying out on the way down.

Who $AMDW is for (and who it isn't)

  • It can make sense if you're bullish on $AMD, want weekly cash flow while you wait, and can stomach a fund that went from $39 to $115 and back down to $58 in about seven months. Think of it as a leveraged $AMD position that spins off cash, not a paycheck replacement.
  • It doesn't fit if you need predictable income. A $0.29 week was followed by a $0.75 week. If you're budgeting off one check, you'll get burned.
  • Reinvesting changes the math. Plowing the checks back in raises your share count, but it also raises your exposure to one very volatile chip stock. Spending every check works fine while $AMD rises. In a drawdown, the payouts shrink and the position shrinks at the same time. I'd decide based on how much $AMD risk you actually want.

For a steadier core, I'd rather build on diversified payers and keep single-stock funds like $AMDW as a small satellite.
